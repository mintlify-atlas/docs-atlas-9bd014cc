Robotic Arm Macondo is a fully open-source, 3D-printed robotic arm and claw that you can build yourself and control wirelessly through a smartphone app. Powered by an Arduino UNO R4 (WiFi variant recommended) and driven by multiple servo motors, the arm uses a 4-bar linkage mechanism for smooth, stable motion and a spur-gear claw for reliable gripping. Everything you need to reproduce it — CAD files, firmware, a bill of materials, and step-by-step assembly instructions — is provided free of charge. Whether you are a student exploring mechatronics or a maker looking for a hands-on servo-control project, Robotic Arm Macondo is designed to be both challenging and deeply rewarding to build.

## What This Project Provides

Robotic Arm Macondo ships with four core deliverables so you can build a complete, working arm from scratch:

- **All CAD files** — 13 STEP files for every 3D-printed component, plus the full OnShape assembly model, so you can inspect, modify, and slice each part.
- **All code** — Arduino firmware for receiving smartphone commands and driving the servo motors with PWM signals.
- **Bill of materials** — a curated, itemised list covering printed parts, servo motors, fasteners, and electronics, sourced from McMaster-Carr, ServoCity, and the Arduino Store.
- **Step-by-step assembly instructions** — detailed guidance that takes you from a pile of printed parts to a fully operational robotic arm.

## What You'll Need

Before you start, gather the following:

- **A 3D printer** — FDM printers with a 0.4 mm nozzle are sufficient for all 13 components; PLA or PETG is recommended.
- **Arduino UNO R4 (WiFi)** — the WiFi variant is strongly recommended for wireless smartphone control over Bluetooth/WiFi. The standard UNO R4 Minima will work for wired testing.
- **Servo motors** — multiple hobby-grade servos (Futaba / HiTec / JR-style, 3-pin PWM connector). Refer to the [Bill of Materials](/bill-of-materials) for exact models and quantities.
- **Structural hardware** — sourced primarily from **McMaster-Carr** (fasteners, bearings, shafts) and **ServoCity** (servo mounts, hubs, and linkage hardware).
- **Electronics hardware** — available from the **Arduino Store** and general electronics suppliers: pin headers, the custom UNO R4 shield PCB, and power connectors.
- **Soldering tools** — a basic soldering iron, solder, and flux are required to assemble the shield and servo connectors.
- **Smartphone** — iOS or Android, used to control the arm through the companion app via the Arduino UNO R4's onboard WiFi/Bluetooth module.

## Key Specifications

| Specification | Detail |
|---|---|
| 3D-printed components | 13 unique parts |
| Microcontroller | Arduino UNO R4 (WiFi recommended) |
| Actuation | Multiple servo motors (PWM-controlled) |
| Arm mechanism | 4-bar linkage |
| Claw mechanism | Spur gear (single motor, dual claw gears) |
| Control interface | Smartphone app (WiFi / Bluetooth) |
| Power supply | Battery → 7.5 V → 5 V / 3.3 V regulated rails |
| CAD platform | OnShape (cloud-based, fully parametric) |

## 3D-Printed Parts

The arm is built from 13 unique printed parts, each serving a specific structural or mechanical role:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Structural Chain" icon="link">
    - **Base** — ground anchor and azimuth rotation
    - **Left Mount** — left shoulder support bracket
    - **Right Mount** — right shoulder support bracket
    - **Bearing Arm** — lower arm segment (shoulder to elbow)
    - **Third Arm** — mid-arm link (elbow to upper assembly)
    - **Top Mount** — upper arm terminus that carries the claw
  </Card>
  <Card title="4-Bar Linkage & Claw" icon="gear">
    - **Servo Arm** — 4-bar rocker/follower link
    - **Top 4 Bar Servo Arm** — 4-bar driven crank link
    - **Top Free Arm** — 4-bar coupler link
    - **Spur Gear** — central drive gear for the claw
    - **Claw Gear Left** — left finger drive gear
    - **Claw Gear Right** — right finger drive gear
    - **Claw Mount** — end-effector housing and gear axle carrier
  </Card>
</CardGroup>
