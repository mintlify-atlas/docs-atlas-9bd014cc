The Robotic Arm Macondo electronics system is built around an Arduino UNO R4 WiFi paired with a custom shield PCB that distributes power and PWM signals to all servo motors. The Arduino's built-in WiFi module handles communication with the smartphone control app over a local network, eliminating the need for any additional wireless hardware. The shield — designed in KiCad and available in the `KiCad Schematics/` directory — sits directly on the Arduino's header pins and provides a bank of 3-pin servo connectors, a 7.5V battery input, and onboard voltage regulation to keep servo power and Arduino logic power cleanly separated.

<Warning>
  Always verify the 7.5V supply is correctly regulated to 5V before connecting servos. Applying unregulated battery voltage directly to servo power inputs will immediately destroy the servo electronics.
</Warning>

## Components Required

Gather the following items before beginning the electronics setup:

- **Arduino UNO R4 WiFi** — WiFi variant is mandatory for smartphone app connectivity
- **Custom shield PCB** — fabricated from the KiCad files in `KiCad Schematics/`, or assembled on protoboard following the schematic
- **Servo motors** (×6) — one per arm joint; standard 3-pin hobby servo connector (PWM / + / −)
- **7.5V battery pack** — connects to the +BATT input on the shield
- **Servo extension / jumper wires** — 3-pin servo connectors at 2.54 mm pitch
- **Male pin headers** — 2.54 mm pitch, to match the Arduino UNO R4 shield footprint

## Power Architecture

The power system uses a layered distribution scheme that keeps high-current servo power separate from sensitive Arduino logic power, reducing noise and protecting the microcontroller.

```
7.5V Battery Pack
       │
   +BATT rail
       │
  Power Switch ──► +VSW (switched battery voltage)
       │
  Voltage Regulators
       ├──► +5V  ─── Servo motor power (all servo + pins)
       └──► +3.3V ── Arduino logic / WiFi module
```

**Key points:**
- The **+BATT** net carries raw battery voltage (7.5V) directly from the battery connector.
- The **+VSW** net is the same voltage passed through a power switch, allowing the system to be safely de-energised without disconnecting the battery.
- The **+5V** net is the regulated servo supply. All servo `+` pins connect here.
- The **+3.3V** net powers the Arduino R4's logic and ESP32-S3 WiFi module.
- **GND** is a shared common reference for all nets.

Keeping servo power on its own +5V regulator is important: servo motors draw large instantaneous currents during movement, which create voltage spikes on shared power rails. Isolating them prevents brownouts that would otherwise reset the Arduino mid-operation.

## Servo Connections

Each servo motor has three wires on a standard 3-pin connector:

| Servo Pin | Signal | Shield Connection |
|---|---|---|
| Pin 1 (usually orange/white) | PWM signal | Arduino digital pin (D2–D13) |
| Pin 2 (usually red) | + power | +5V regulated rail |
| Pin 3 (usually brown/black) | Ground | GND |

The KiCad schematic (`uno-r4-shield-template.kicad_sch`) routes Arduino digital pins D2 through D13 to individual `Conn_01x03` (3-pin) servo connectors on the shield. This provides up to 12 servo channels — more than sufficient for the 6 joints on Robotic Arm Macondo.

### Servo-to-Pin Assignment

| Servo # | Joint | Arduino Digital Pin |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Base rotation | D2 |
| 2 | Shoulder (Bearing Arm) | D3 |
| 3 | Elbow (Third Arm) | D4 |
| 4 | Wrist / Top linkage | D5 |
| 5 | Claw open/close (Spur Gear) | D6 |
| 6 | Claw rotation | D7 |

<Note>
  The pin assignments above are a recommended starting configuration. You can remap any servo to any digital pin D2–D13 by modifying the firmware. Analog pins A0/D14 through A5/D19 are available on the shield headers for sensors or additional actuators if your design expands.
</Note>

## Shield PCB

The custom shield PCB was designed with KiCad 10 and is stored in the `KiCad Schematics/` directory of the repository. It is titled **"Arduino UNO R4 Shield Template"** (Rev 1A, 2023-08-26) and includes:

- Multiple `Conn_01x03` 3-pin servo headers routed to D2–D13
- +BATT battery input connector
- +VSW switched power net
- +5V and +3.3V regulated power distribution
- GND plane
- Four `MountingHole_3.2mm_M3` PCB mounting holes (H1–H4) that align with the Base part's mounting boss pattern

For full schematic reference and PCB layout details, see the [KiCad schematic page](/electronics/schematic).

## Initial Electronics Setup

<Steps>
  <Step title="Assemble or order the shield PCB">
    Open the KiCad project in `KiCad Schematics/uno-r4-shield-template.kicad_pro`. Export Gerber files and send to a PCB fabrication service, or replicate the schematic on protoboard if a quick breadboard prototype is preferred.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Install headers matching the Arduino UNO R4 footprint">
    Solder male 2.54 mm pin headers on the shield in the positions that correspond to the Arduino UNO R4 digital, analog, power, and communication headers. Verify header alignment using the Arduino UNO R4 pin diagram before soldering.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect servo signal wires to digital pins">
    Plug each servo's PWM (signal) wire into its assigned `Conn_01x03` header on the shield. Refer to the servo-to-pin assignment table above. Ensure the connector orientation is correct — most servo connectors are keyed, but double-check the white/orange signal wire is on the signal pin side.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect servo power to the 5V rail">
    Verify that the + (red) wire of each servo connector is landing on the +5V trace, not the signal trace. The shield's servo connectors are wired in the standard order: PWM, +5V, GND from top to bottom.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect the battery to the +BATT input">
    Connect your 7.5V battery pack to the +BATT input connector on the shield. Do not power on yet — complete the next step first.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify voltage levels before powering on">
    With the power switch in the OFF (+VSW disconnected) position, use a multimeter to confirm that the +5V regulator output reads 5.0V (±0.1V) and the +3.3V output reads 3.3V (±0.1V) before the Arduino and servos are connected. Only after confirming correct regulation should you seat the shield onto the Arduino and connect servo cables.
  </Step>
</Steps>
